# Audit: car models, performance, architecture

Date: 2026-11-19. Measured in the dev build (Chromium in the Claude desktop app, Mac, device pixel ratio 2, canvas 722 x 713 CSS px).

## 1. Car models

### What they are

The three cars (saloon, off-roader, supercar) are generated by Python scripts in Blender (`art/`): a control cage of cross-sections, a subdivision surface, boolean wheel arches, then panels cut by material, with gap and flange panels. Nothing is modelled by hand.

| Car | Triangles | Meshes in game | Shadow casters | Deformable meshes | Materials | GLB size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saloon | 149k | 108 | 27 | 84 | 31 | 1.13 MB |
| Off-roader | 144k | 106 | 26 | 82 | 29 | 0.96 MB |
| Supercar | 163k | 97 | 24 | 73 | 29 | 1.39 MB |

### What is wrong with them

Looked at from three-quarter views in the game:

- **Shapes are soft and generic.** A subdivided cage gives rounded, inflated volumes. There are no crisp feature lines, no real surface transitions, and the cars do not read as a specific type (the saloon is not an Evo-style rally saloon).
- **Floating and detached details.** Mirrors hover off the doors, the supercar's wing sits on thin posts in the air, the saloon's A-pillar trim reads as a blue bar across the windscreen.
- **Holes and see-through areas.** The supercar's side intakes and the saloon's grille show through to the inside or end in visible edges.
- **Proportions.** The saloon's tail is long and heavy, the off-roader's nose is flat and box-like without detail, the wheels are a little small for their arches.
- **Materials.** 29 to 31 materials per car split every part into several meshes: about 100 draw calls per car.
- **Cost.** 145k to 163k triangles per car is a lot for what is on screen, and every triangle of the deformable panels is re-skinned by the crash deformation.

Conclusion: the procedural pipeline was a good way to get cars with correct part structure for crashes, but it does not produce good-looking cars. Better results come from a hand-made model, reworked into our part structure.

### Plan

1. Take freely licensed models made by people (licence must allow redistribution in a public repo: CC0 or CC-BY). Candidates are listed in section 4.
2. Rework each one in Blender: split into the parts the crash system needs (body, bonnet, boot, four doors, bumpers, glass panes, lights, mirrors, wheels), set hinge pivots, remove logos, reduce materials to about 8 (paint, glass, chrome, black trim, rubber, rim, lights, interior), aim for 40k to 80k triangles.
3. Keep the crash physics the same: crush rays are measured on the new shells, the deformation lattice binds to the new meshes.

## 2. Performance

### Measured

| | Test area | City | Grand Prix circuit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Render (CPU + GPU, synced) | 9.5 ms | 5.8 ms | 5.8 ms |
| One physics step, all cars and props | 0.24 ms | 0.23 ms | 0.23 ms |
| Draw calls (with the shadow pass) | 554 | 443 | 484 |

In the city, hiding the three cars takes the render from 5.8 ms to 2.4 ms; hiding the whole map only to 4.9 ms. **The cars are the main rendering cost**, because of their draw calls (about 100 each, plus the shadow pass) and their triangle count.

Halving the resolution only saved 1.2 ms on this machine (draw-call bound), but on a bigger window or a weaker GPU the pixel count dominates: at device pixel ratio 2 a full-screen 1440 x 900 window renders 5.2 million pixels.

### Findings

| # | Problem | Impact | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Pixel ratio up to 2 on high-DPI screens | Up to 4x the pixel work | Fixed: capped at 1.5 and adaptive (down to 0.6 when frames run long); garage setting to pin it |
| P2 | About 100 draw calls and 150k triangles per car, 3 cars always drawn at full detail | Main render cost | Planned: new models with about 8 materials and 40k to 80k triangles |
| P3 | Normals of every dented mesh recomputed in one go | About 40 ms stall on a big crash frame | Fixed: vertex budget per frame, round-robin |
| P4 | `all()` built a new array 3 times per physics step | Garbage-collector pauses | Fixed: one stable array |
| P5 | Street furniture sync built a `Set` every frame | Garbage | Fixed |
| P6 | Lap panel text rewritten every frame | Layout work | Fixed: 20 Hz |
| P7 | Each car has its own particle pools (sparks, smoke, tyre smoke, skids) | 12 draw calls and buffers for 3 cars | Acceptable; shared pool is a later step |
| P8 | Physics (0.23 ms per 120 Hz step) | Fine | - |

## 3. Architecture

| # | Problem | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | `main.ts` held everything: boot, garage wiring, frame loop, lap logic, teleports, debug API | Fixed: `main.ts` only builds systems; `Game` owns the loop, cars and actions; `RaceSystem` owns lap timing |
| A2 | Ground surface was a module-level global set by the map | Fixed: injected through the physics world (`physics.surfaceAt`) |
| A3 | Vehicle code imported the world module (surface grip) | Fixed: surface types and grip live in `core/physics` |
| A4 | `Vehicle.ts` is 850 lines (steering, assists, drivetrain, suspension, tyres in one step function) | Open: split into steps once the new cars are in |
| A5 | Car configs are JSON cast to the type without checks | Fixed: `tests/config.test.ts` validates every car |

Layering after the changes: `core` (math, physics, input, loop) <- `vehicle` and `damage` (simulation, no rendering) <- `render`, `world`, `ui` <- `game` (orchestration) <- `main` (boot).

Tests: 64 headless tests (driving, crashes, damage, handling, garage, map, lap timer, kerbs) run in about 20 s.
